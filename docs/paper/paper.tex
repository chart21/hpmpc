\begin{abstract}
We describe the optimizations and fixes that make HPMPC's two-party ABY2 inference of ResNet50 fast,
reproducible and correct across all of its protocol variants. The work targets both communication and
runtime, in the preprocessing phase (homomorphic encryption for linear layers, silent OT for Boolean
material) and in the online phase (round latency and per-round work). For one ImageNet image,
preprocessing drops from 17.1\s to 4.3\s on two AMD EPYC 7543 hosts (3.3\s on EPYC 9354) and
its triple traffic from 1112 to 587\,MiB. The online phase drops from 2.4--4.3\s to 0.70\s.
BatchNorm triples shrink from 5.7\,GB to 0.19\,GB of traffic per
10 CIFAR-10 images, and Beaver 3- and 4-tuples need half the OTs and about 45\% less traffic.
Evaluating several conv-triple chunks at once halves the CIFAR-10 conv phase, and in multi-batch mode the eight
lanes of a register share one convolution, which cuts the conv phase per process from 8.6 to 3.9\s and the
preprocessing traffic by half.
On CIFAR-10 we evaluate 30 protocol variants (three MSB adders, five ReLU families, fused and unfused
BatchNorm) in single-batch and 192-image multi-batch mode on two machine pairs. On the EPYC 9354 pair, preprocessing
takes 1.3--2.7\s per 10 images and the online phase 0.35--0.50\s; 192 images take 7.8--19.5\s of preprocessing.
Public weights, which need no linear-layer triples, and fully shared weight masks (\flag{A_KNOWN=0}) work in every variant.
With the AdamW-trained ResNet50, every variant classifies 62--72 of 100 images (plaintext 76) and
118--131 of 192 (61--68\%; plaintext 71.9\%). Along the way we fixed a thread race, two connection hangs, four protocol
bugs that broke whole variant families, and the sources of run-to-run nondeterminism: all 60 threaded builds
reproduced their serial reference bit for bit, and the final single-batch builds still give the same outputs.
For the artifact's 36 single-batch all\_opt configurations on ImageNet, preprocessing gets $2.9$--$7.1\times$
faster with secret weights and the online phase $2.0$--$3.0\times$ (EPYC 9354). Their A2bits configurations
were not correct as given; the A2B bake that fixes them commits every ReLU input's mask before the preprocessing pass,
for free behind convolutions and residual sums and through a mask-only forward with public weights, and an optional
output repacking cuts the conv triples' traffic by 40\%. On a GPU, the packed pipeline's evaluator produces the conv
triples $2.1$--$2.3\times$ faster than the CPU and $5.6$--$7.0\times$ faster than the previous GPU path. With ferret's
extensions and the random OTs' hashing on the GPU as well, bit for bit emp's, preprocessing on one machine shared by
both parties is $1.7$--$2.2\times$ faster than on its CPU in single batch and $2.1$--$2.4\times$ in multi-batch, and the
online conv products build their column matrix on the GPU.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two-party inference with a preprocessing phase splits the cost of a secure neural network into two
parts. An input-independent \emph{preprocessing phase} produces correlated randomness: multiplication
triples for the linear layers, generated with homomorphic encryption (HE), and Boolean triples and
tuples for the nonlinear layers, generated with oblivious transfer (OT). The \emph{online phase} then
evaluates the network on masked values and needs only a few messages per layer. HPMPC implements this
design with an ABY2-style protocol~\cite{aby2} (\flag{PROTOCOL=4}) and generates its preprocessing
material with the CHEETAH~\cite{cheetah} HE and silent-OT~\cite{ferret} machinery (ConvTriple).

This report collects the engineering work that took this pipeline from a working prototype to a fast
and reproducible system. Its focus is communication and runtime; \cref{fig:summary} summarizes the largest effects.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{HE preprocessing} (\cref{sec:he}): a packed and pipelined convolution evaluator, an exact
  wire format that sends only the bits decryption needs, and BatchNorm triples packed into full
  ciphertexts and generated for all layers in one product, several chunks evaluated at once, and in multi-batch
  mode one convolution for all lanes. ImageNet preprocessing: $17.1\s \to 4.3\s$ ($4.0\times$).
\item \textbf{Boolean preprocessing} (\cref{sec:ot}): OT packs sized by total demand, ferret parameters and
  a prefetching LPN step, \flag{TCP_NODELAY} on every channel, bit-packed random OTs, and Beaver tuples
  whose products share one OT per common factor (half the OTs, ${\approx}45\%$ less traffic).
\item \textbf{Online phase} (\cref{sec:online}): Nagle-free sockets, element-parallel circuit levels for
  batch size one, pooled GEMM threads, an ahead-of-time PRNG and fewer rounds per ReLU. ImageNet online: $2.4\text{--}4.3\s \to 0.70\s$.
\item \textbf{Correctness and reproducibility} (\cref{sec:correct}): deterministic OT and HE randomness,
  and fixes for a worker race, lost wake-ups, stale listeners, multi-batch A2B, the threaded GEMM, and the
  BatchNorm of \flag{A_KNOWN=0} and of public weights.
\item \textbf{Evaluation} (\cref{sec:eval}): 30 variants $\times$ 2 batch modes $\times$ 2 machine pairs,
  with final accuracy on a trained model.
\item \textbf{The all\_opt configurations on ImageNet} (\cref{sec:triad}): 36 builds over three weight settings, two
  ReLU protocols and three adders. The A2bits configurations as given compute wrong signs; the A2B bake fixes them
  by committing every ReLU input's mask before the preprocessing pass, at no traffic behind convolutions and residual
  sums. The conv triples start alongside the OT phase. Preprocessing $2.9\text{--}7.1\times$ faster with secret weights,
  online $2.0\text{--}3.0\times$.
\item \textbf{GPU} (\cref{sec:gpu}): a GPU evaluator inside the packed conv pipeline, and ferret's LPN step, GGM trees
  and the random OTs' hashing on the GPU, all bit-identical to the CPU's. Conv triples $2.1\text{--}2.3\times$ faster;
  end to end, on one shared machine, preprocessing $1.7\text{--}2.2\times$ faster than on the CPU, $2.1\text{--}2.4\times$ in
  multi-batch; the online conv products on the GPU with the column matrix built there.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{Protocol.} ABY2 shares a value $v$ as a public masked value $m = v + \lambda$ together with
additive shares of the mask $\lambda = \lambda_0 + \lambda_1$. Every mask is fixed in preprocessing, so a
linear layer is local except for one message that re-masks its output, and its preprocessing is a triple
over the masks, e.g.\ $[\lambda_w \cdot \lambda_x]$ for a convolution (\cref{fig:overview}). With
\flag{A_KNOWN=1} the model owner P0 holds the weight masks and the data owner P1 the activation masks
(the AB2 triple); with \flag{A_KNOWN=0} both are shared (AB). We name the three weight settings by who knows
the weights: \emph{UC1}, known to none (secret-shared, \flag{A_KNOWN=0}); \emph{UC2}, known to one party, the model
owner (\flag{A_KNOWN=1}); \emph{UC3}, known to all (public weights, \flag{PUBLIC_WEIGHTS=1}, no linear-layer
triples). A ReLU converts its input to Boolean
shares (A2B), extracts the sign with an MSB adder and injects the bit back into the arithmetic domain
(bit injection). The adder sets the number of online rounds per ReLU: the ripple-carry adder
(RCA) needs 27 AND levels, the parallel-prefix adders PPA and PPA4 (4-input prefix gates, which use
Beaver 3- and 4-tuples) need fewer levels but more AND gates.

\paragraph{Variants.} We measure three adders (RCA, PPA, PPA4) and five ReLU families: \emph{plain};
\emph{reshare} (\flag{RESHARE_OPT}); \emph{reshare+sim}, which folds the reshare material into the
convolution mask (\flag{RESHARE_OPT_SIM}); \emph{a2b}, which moves the A2B to preprocessing and bakes its
mask into the convolution output (\flag{A2B_ONLINE_OPT}, \flag{A2B_CONV_BAKE}); and \emph{a2b+AKTE}, an
adder for inputs whose mask the evaluators know (\flag{A_KNOWN_TO_EVALUATORS_OPT}). Each variant runs with
BatchNorm fused into the preceding convolution or unfused. All variants share \flag{BITLENGTH=32},
\flag{FRACTIONAL=5}, probabilistic (SecureML-style) truncation, \flag{FUSE_RELU_AVG=1} and
\flag{CUT_FRACTIONAL_BITS_OPT=1}.

\paragraph{Workloads.} ResNet50 on CIFAR-10 in two modes. \emph{Single batch}: one process,
32-bit lanes (\flag{DATTYPE=32}), 10 images, 32 HE/OT threads and 31 extra online threads.
\emph{Multi-batch}: 24 processes with 256-bit registers of eight 32-bit lanes, 192 images, 3 HE/OT
threads and one extra online thread per process. The HE preprocessing study and the online steps use ResNet50 on one
ImageNet image, \flag{FUNCTION_IDENTIFIER=87}: CHEETAH's ResNet50 layout for 1000 classes, whose $224\times224$ input
arrives padded to $230\times230$. It runs with a dummy model and image; since no code path depends on the values
(\cref{sec:zero}), the times are those of a trained model. \Cref{tab:imagenet} lists every flag that differs from the
defaults in \texttt{config.h}.

\paragraph{Measurement.} Preprocessing and online time are P0's wall-clock times (the maximum over
processes in multi-batch). Single-batch numbers are medians of two runs; multi-batch runs once.
All randomness is seeded, so every run of a build produces a
bit-identical output; we check each run's output hash against a serial build without extra threads
(\cref{sec:correct}). The runtime does not depend on the weights: HE triples are generated with
\flag{TRIPLE_ZERO=ON} and no code path skips zero values.
Accuracy uses two trained ResNet50 models: \texttt{Cifar\_adam\_001} for the runtime matrix and an
AdamW-trained model (74.35\% on the test set) for the final accuracy.

\paragraph{Packed evaluator (s1, s2).} CHEETAH's convolution encrypts one input tile per ciphertext and
returns one ciphertext per output channel and tile. The packed layout of the GPU path puts several images
and output channels into each ciphertext: triple traffic falls from 1112 to 698\,MiB. At first this made the evaluator
slower (s1), because every weight polynomial still took a full NTT. The evaluator now transforms a weight
polynomial right before its only use, and it transforms $1\times1$ weights with NTTs of size
$N/2^a$. It builds sparse polynomials from a table of powers and sums products lazily in 128 bits.
Convolution time falls from 13.7 to 7.5\s.

\paragraph{Randomness and threads (s3--s6).} Flooding noise and masks come from an AES-CTR generator
(AES-NI) instead of SEAL's Blake2; the thread pool survives across the several parallel calls of a layer;
the multiplexer and COT triples run on all threads. Together these steps take preprocessing from 11.9 to 5.4\s.

\paragraph{Pipelining (s8).} Per layer, each party idled while the other worked: at s7 P0 waited
0.82\s and P1 1.95\s in total. The pipelined protocol (\cref{fig:pipeline}) overlaps P1's
encryption and decryption with P0's evaluation. It sends exactly the same bytes and cuts convolution
time from 2.6 to 1.9\s; with \flag{A_KNOWN=0}, where both parties encrypt, from 3.9--4.0 to 2.0\s in
isolation.

\paragraph{Concurrent evaluation.} Even pipelined, AB2 was slower than AB on CIFAR-10: 0.34--0.39\s against
0.26--0.30\s for the 23 convolutions of the isolated test, although in AB each party evaluates a full convolution under HE.
Evaluating one chunk (up to 16 images) is a sequence of parallel regions: loading the inputs, the multiply-accumulate over
blocks of output groups and primes, and masking, flooding and truncating the outputs. On CIFAR's small layers these regions
have fewer tasks than threads. An AB party keeps its threads busy by overlapping encryption, evaluation and decryption;
the AB2 weight holder evaluated one chunk after another and sent each result before starting the next (\cref{fig:concurrent}).
Now three evaluator threads take the chunks in order and a sender thread emits the outputs in chunk order. Chunk $k$
draws its masks and flooding noise from the PRNG call that the serial loop gave it, so the triples are the same bits
and every output hash is unchanged. \Cref{tab:concurrent} shows the effect of the number of chunks in flight. AB got the same change.
End to end, the conv phase of a 10-image preprocessing halves, from 0.54--0.65 to 0.26--0.31\s.

\paragraph{Exact wire format (s9).} SEAL serializes a seeded input ciphertext with zstd to 62.4\,KB. P1
now sends only a 16-byte seed and $c_0$ at exactly $60+49=109$ bits per coefficient (55.8\,KB). P0
expands $c_1$ from the seed directly in NTT form, which saves an inverse and a forward NTT per input. For
outputs, the modulus switch before decryption keeps 34 bits of $c_0$ and 46 of $c_1$; exactly those bits
travel, 20--25\% less than zstd. The tile search now minimizes bytes rather than ciphertexts, since an
input costs about 2.4 times an output. Convolution traffic falls 18\% and all preprocessing traffic 12\%
(\cref{tab:traffic}); decrypted values are bit-identical, and a check rejects outputs whose dropped bits are
not zero. The 109-bit input modulus is a floor: BFV's plaintext product carries a term of up to
${\approx}2^{74}$.

\paragraph{BatchNorm triples.} Unfused BatchNorm multiplies each activation by a per-channel scale, which
the slot-encoded protocol computes as an elementwise product of two vectors. The original code split each image's
elements among the 32 threads before packing: every thread sent its own nearly empty ciphertexts, one image at
a time. Packing all images into full ciphertexts cut BN traffic from 2704 / 3029 to 90 / 101\,MB and BN time from
6.2--7.9 to 0.7--0.8\s (CIFAR-10, 10 images). Each of the 53 BN layers was still a separate call, and one
call is latency-bound (1.6--6.4\,MB in 13--50\ms). \flag{CHEETAH_BN_BATCHED} concatenates all slot-encoded
layers into one product, split among the threads in whole ciphertexts: the BN phase falls from 0.75 to
0.19\s with the same traffic (\cref{tab:bn}).

\paragraph{Value independence.}\label{sec:zero} The benchmark's dummy model makes every weight mask zero, and early
versions of the packed evaluator skipped zero weights, so their benchmark times looked 0.6--2.9\s
faster. With weights known in preprocessing, a zero-skipping evaluator would also leak the number of zero weights through its
timing. All evaluators now do the same work for any weight values, and all numbers in this report are value-independent.

\subsection{Conv triples and OT on a GPU}\label{sec:gpu}

ConvTriple's GPU path (\texttt{TRIPLE\_GPU}, troy-nova) evaluates each convolution with troy's \texttt{Conv2dHelper}, one
round trip per layer, full ciphertexts on the wire. The packed CPU evaluator started from its tiling (s1) and then went
further: the exact wire format, the pipeline across layers, concurrent evaluation, the start alongside the OT phase and the
repacking variant. The GPU path got none of these. Now the packed pipeline itself has a GPU evaluator (\texttt{TRIPLE\_GPU}
builds, \texttt{CONV\_GPU=0} turns it off), so all of them carry over. The device builds each weight polynomial directly in
the shared memory of its NTT kernel (every coefficient, zero or a weight, computed from the weight tensor: no memset and
no extra pass), transforms the inputs' $c_0$, sums the products with 128-bit accumulators (both halves of a ciphertext per
thread, so each weight value is loaded once) and transforms the results back. The host keeps parsing the inputs,
flooding, masking, truncation and the wire format, through pinned staging buffers. The transforms replicate SEAL's
\texttt{ntt\_negacyclic\_harvey} with SEAL's own tables and slot order. So $c_1$, which arrives in SEAL's NTT form, needs no
conversion, the triples are the same values as on the CPU, and the two-party check of all 53 ImageNet convolutions passes
exactly.

\paragraph{The conv triples alone.} \Cref{tab:gpu} compares CPU and GPU on one workstation, the only machine with a GPU,
so both parties share its CPU. In isolation the GPU evaluator produces the conv triples $2.3\times$ (AB2) and $2.1\times$

the whole inference that alone changes little (0.15--1.40\s): on this machine preprocessing is the OT phase. With 16
one-channel OT packs per party, setting them up takes 2.0--2.5\s, the A2bits builds' Boolean addition (31 dependent COT
rounds) another 4.8--5.1\s, and the conv triples (4.6--6.4\s on the CPU, 1.4--2.3\s on the GPU) run alongside.

\paragraph{The OT phase on the GPU.} Ferret's extensions now run on the GPU as well, in four steps
(\cref{tab:gpu}, middle). (1) The LPN step: ten AES-indexed gathers per output, from a table of
$k = 238{,}000$ blocks that stays in the GPU's L2 cache; each lane keeps its own copy of the AES T-table in shared memory,
so the table lookups never conflict. (2) MPCOT: all 2{,}507 GGM trees of an extension are built level by level, the
sender's from emp's seeds, the receiver's from its OT messages, with the per-level XOR sums reduced across warps. The LPN
step then runs on the leaves in place, and one copy returns the outputs. Each channel's tree messages leave as one
message, the same bytes as emp's 2{,}507 separate flushes. (3) The outputs stay on the GPU. In the ImageNet runs, 97\% of
all COTs go to random OTs with 1-bit messages (or $k$ bit planes), which hash each COT with emp's MITCCRH (a fresh AES
key per OT) and keep one bit or a few. The GPU hashes them in place, scheduling each key on the fly, and returns only the
bit planes: two to six bits per OT cross PCIe instead of 128, and the CPU does no hashing. The other consumers get host copies of their
range on demand. (4) With extensions this cheap, setting up the packs dominated, so a GPU build uses 4 packs of 4
channels instead of 16 of 1: setup 0.8--1.2\s, preprocessing 0.6--1.3\s lower. Every step yields emp's values bit for
bit. Check modes compare every tree, output and hashed bit with emp's CPU code. The Boolean triples pass the two-party
tuple test, and seeded CIFAR-10 ResNet50 runs give the CPU build's output hashes (with the same pack layout). The bytes
on the wire do not change, so either party may run either path. Each ferret instance keeps its 164\,MiB of outputs on
the device; with both parties on one 20\,GB GPU there is room.

\paragraph{End to end.} With conv triples and OT on the GPU, preprocessing is $2.2\times$ faster than on the CPU in the
A2bits builds and $1.7$--$1.8\times$ in the reshared ones (\cref{tab:gpu}, bottom). What remains of preprocessing on the
CPU is the packs' setup (base OTs, IKNP and each pack's first extension), the A2bits builds' Boolean addition
(latency-bound COT rounds), the 3\% of COTs whose consumers need the blocks themselves, and the HE work around the GPU
evaluator (encryption, flooding, decryption). HPMPC's own CUTLASS convolution (\flag{USE_CUDA_GEMM=2}) bypasses the
per-output mask step and with it the A2B bake.

\paragraph{The online conv layers.} A step timer over the online phase shows where a conv layer's time goes (one
ImageNet image, P0): 0.08\s of 0.21\s in the products, 0.05\s in the per-output mask and send, 0.03\s zeroing the outputs
before the products, 0.03\s adding the bias, 0.02\s in im2col and 0.01\s copying the bias masks for the A2B bake to every
output. The zeroing and the bias add were serial loops and now run on the GEMM threads; the bake takes one bias mask per
channel. For the products, \flag{GEMM_FAST_GPU} sent the im2col'd operand, nine times the input for a $3\times3$ kernel,
over PCIe and gained nothing. Now the GPU builds the column matrix itself from the input feature map (only the input
travels, and the host's im2col is skipped), multiplies with the existing exact CUTLASS uint32 GEMM, and returns the
result. The output hashes are unchanged. With the parallel loops, the conv layers' online time falls from about 0.21 to
0.15--0.18\s on the CPU and to 0.13--0.14\s with the GPU product, and the UC2 online phase of the GPU builds matches the
CPU's (0.425 against 0.426\s, \cref{tab:gpumb}). The UC1 builds (\flag{A_KNOWN=0}) do not use \flag{GEMM_FAST}; their online
phase measured 0.04--0.05\s higher with the GPU OT path. That gap is still unexplained: pinning, a 5\,s pause before the
online phase, OT work during the online phase (there is none) and memory pressure are ruled out.

\subsection{Engineering the OT preprocessing}

The ReLUs consume AND triples, Beaver 3- and 4-tuples (PPA4), COT triples (bit injection) and multiplexer
triples. All come from ferret silent OT, 32 OT packs of two ferret instances each.

\paragraph{Ferret parameters and the LPN step.} With 32 instances at once, ferret's LPN step is
memory-bound on its 7.2\,MB tables. Computing the AES of the next four groups ahead and prefetching their table rows
cuts the step from 0.34 to 0.28\s with bit-identical outputs. Parameter set b12 has a 3.8\,MB table that
stays in cache, for 6.5\,MB more setup traffic per direction. Key setup falls from 1.42 to 1.13\s. VAES did not help:
the LPN step is memory-bound and MITCCRH is bound by its AES key schedule.

\paragraph{Demand-sized OT packs.} The first OT consumer used to size the packs, and later consumers
extended them in small steps. hpmpc now passes the total OT demand of the whole preprocessing to ConvTriple
(\texttt{ot\_demand\_hint}) before the first generation. On CIFAR-10 the OT-pack phase falls from 0.91 to 0.47\s,
and PPA4, reshare and a2b get enough capacity for their tuples.

\paragraph{\flag{TCP_NODELAY} everywhere.} The ConvTriple channels enabled \flag{TCP_NODELAY} only in WAN
mode. The COT multiply behind the a2b bake flushes small messages 31 times in sequence, and each flush waited
for a delayed ACK. One call took 170\ms; with Nagle off it takes 11\ms. PPA a2b single-batch
preprocessing falls from 8.4 to 3.5\s, PPA4 reshare+sim from 8.9 to 5.0\s. The same fix on hpmpc's own
sockets takes the ImageNet online phase from 2.4--4.3 to 1.9--2.2\s (\cref{sec:online}).

\paragraph{Bit-packed random OTs.} Random OTs with one-bit messages are hashed and reduced to bits in
cache-sized chunks and stored eight per byte (\texttt{send/recv\_rot\_bits}), where the generic
interface kept two 16-byte blocks per OT. AND triples take 0.36 instead of 0.50\s, and the COT multiply
reads the same bits with far less memory traffic.

\paragraph{Beaver 3- and 4-tuples with shared OTs.} Before this work, a 3-tuple was an AND triple plus three
independent correlated-OT multiplications $a\cdot c$, $b\cdot c$ and $ab\cdot c$, each with its own random OT per

fresh $c$ from the OT choices (\cref{fig:outer}). A 3-tuple now needs 4 random OTs instead of 8 and a 4-tuple 10
instead of 22 (\cref{tab:otcost}). In a multi-batch PPA4 run the tuple phases fall from 19.0 to 9.2\s per process
and their traffic by 43--46\% (\cref{fig:tuples}). Unit tests check all products; ReLU exactness tests report 0 wrong

\section{Online phase}\label{sec:online}

On a 0.3\ms LAN, the online phase is bound by round latency and per-round work, not by bandwidth.
Activations send about 180\,MB per ImageNet inference, 0.06\s at 25\,Gbit/s. \Cref{fig:online}
shows the steps for one ImageNet image.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering\input{figures/online_imagenet}
  \caption{Online phase of one ImageNet image on flare / polynize (ranges over runs where they varied).
  Output hashes are unchanged by every step.}
  \label{fig:online}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Nagle, buffers and GEMM threads.} With Nagle's algorithm, a round's last small message waited for a delayed
ACK. ReLU layers were bimodal (6 or 46\ms), and the online phase varied between 2.4 and 4.3\s. With
\flag{TCP_NODELAY} it takes 1.9--2.2\s. Send and receive buffers of 100\,000 words mean fewer messages
and thread hand-offs per round. The threaded GEMM first sent its outputs in linear order while
the completion visits $64\times64$ tiles; CIFAR-10 accuracy was 0\% until both used the tile order. Its threads now live in a
lock-free pool.

\paragraph{Element-parallel levels at batch size one.} HPMPC vectorizes only across independent inputs,
so a single image evaluated every level of a ReLU (adder steps, A2B, bit injection) element by
element on one thread. Within a level, every element runs the same code and consumes the same number of
words from each stream: sends, receives, preprocessed values, its own random words and the indices into
the triple buffers. \texttt{stream\_parallel\_for} measures this consumption on the first element and runs
the rest on the worker pool, each worker with cursors at exactly the stream positions the serial loop
would use (\cref{fig:stream}). Segments end at send/receive buffer boundaries, and a misuse check verifies
every worker's consumption. The messages, their order and all values are those of the serial loop.
With bulk random words, parallel adder construction and the GEMM mask-and-send and completion on the same
pool, the ImageNet ReLU time falls from 630 to 300\ms and the online phase from 1.09 to 0.70\s.
\flag{RNG_AHEAD} moves the party's own sequential AES stream to a background thread.

\paragraph{Rounds per ReLU.} What remains is round latency: 1,752 rounds for CIFAR-10 with RCA against 776
with PPA and 625 with PPA4 (\cref{tab:cut}). \flag{CUT_FRACTIONAL_BITS_OPT} removes the fractional bits
from the MSB adder by substituting identity elements and public constants. RCA loses 245 of its 1,997
rounds (12\%), and ReLU traffic falls 8--11\% for every adder. Preprocessing and accuracy do not change
measurably (1,134 vs.\ 1,138 of 1,752 evaluations correct with and without CUT).

\section{Multi-batch}\label{sec:multi}

With 24 processes of 256-bit lanes, preprocessing dominates. With more than one HE/OT thread per process,
the processes shared connections: channel $i$ used port $\mathit{base} + i\cdot\mathit{PROCESS\_NUM}$ while
process bases stepped by 3, which caused hangs and wrong triples. With a stride of $3\cdot\mathit{PROCESS\_NUM}$, preprocessing falls from
25.0 to 14.0\s with three threads per process (\cref{tab:multi}). One extra online thread per process cuts
the online phase from 0.81 to 0.67\s with an identical output hash.

\paragraph{Lanes in one convolution.} Each 256-bit register holds eight images (lanes), and the conv triples were
generated as one single-image convolution per lane and layer, each with its own copy of the weight masks: 424 small,
unpipelined convolutions per process, with ciphertexts that are mostly empty. The model owner's mask of a weight is $-w$
(\flag{SHARE_PREP}), and every lane's image uses the same weights, so all lanes have the same weight masks by construction.
The eight triples of a layer are therefore one convolution over eight images with one set of weights.
\flag{CHEETAH_CONV_LANES} generates them that way and passes all layers to one pipelined call. A debug check confirmed
that no lane's masks differ in any layer or process. With three HE/OT threads per process the conv phase falls from 8.4--8.7 to 3.8--3.9\s
and P0's preprocessing traffic by 50--62\% (\cref{tab:lanes}); four threads, which enable the pipeline, gain nothing more.
With the AdamW model the accuracy is unchanged (PPA 121 of 192 against 123 without, RCA 131, PPA4 127).
The premise does not depend on \flag{A_KNOWN}: input sharing gives the model owner the share $-w$ and the other party 0 in
every lane either way, and \flag{A_KNOWN=0} only turns the triple into an AB triple, for which both parties encrypt their
shares. The lane batching therefore also applies to \flag{A_KNOWN=0}. In a local test (one process, 8 lanes of 4 images,
4 HE threads, AdamW model) the lane check stays silent for both parties, the conv phase falls from 12.9 to 7.2\s and the
conv traffic from 1036 to 540\,MB per direction, and 22 against 23 of 32 images are classified correctly. On flare /
polynize (24 processes, 192 images) the conv phase per process falls from 9.7--10.0 to 4.1--4.4\s and preprocessing by
a factor of 1.28--1.67 over the 30 variants (\cref{sec:kpw}).

\paragraph{Weights known in preprocessing.} The final ImageNet builds (\cref{sec:triad}) set
\flag{MODELWEIGHTS_KNOWN_DURING_PREPROCESSING}, and in multi-batch three of their single-batch optimizations were off. The
lane batching excluded it: P1's conv-triple shares are prescribed, and the lanes' product now puts them in layer by
layer after the batched product, as the single-batch pipeline does. Since the prescribed shares fix the triples, the
outputs equal the per-layer path's bit for bit. The A2B bake excluded it because its prescribed shares treated a
register as one word; they are now computed lane by lane (\cref{sec:correct}). And the conv triples ran after the
preprocessing pass, before the next generators. They now run on four channels of their own
(\flag{CHEETAH_CONV_SIDE}) alongside the Boolean addition, COT and multiplexer triples. The online products take all
lanes as one GEMM with eight times the columns (\flag{GEMM_FAST} for \flag{DATTYPE} $> 32$: every lane holds the same
weights, checked per layer). On the workstation (one ImageNet process per party, 8 images, UC2 A2bits RCA) the conv
phase falls from 16.0 to 5.3\s on the GPU and from 20.7 to 15.4\s on the CPU, and preprocessing from 49.1 to 33.0\s and
from 86.8 to 79.3\s (\cref{sec:gpu}).

\section{Correctness and reproducibility}\label{sec:correct}

Fast code is only useful if its outputs are right and repeatable. We made every run bit-reproducible, compared each
threaded build against a serial one and bisected every configuration that lost accuracy over the added
optimizations; this exposed the following bugs.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Nondeterministic OT.} COT and multiplexer triple shares are the ferret outputs, and hpmpc
  truncates shares locally, so their per-run randomness changed the outputs. ConvTriple's PRGs are now seeded per party, stage, call and
  task, and a scoped override seeds emp's default PRGs per OT pack and worker (\texttt{DetSeedScope}).
\item \textbf{Worker race in P0's A2B.} For the reshare+sim skip with PPA4, P0 chooses each group's slice
  masks from the Beaver 3-tuples that the group's adder will consume, found through a count of groups prepared so far. On
  the worker pool that count was one shared global. Threaded PPA4 reshare+sim with fused BN gave a different
  output every run. The count is now a per-worker stream index. The race stayed hidden whenever the pool threads woke one after another.
\item \textbf{Hangs.} A lost wake-up at connection start, where flag and broadcast were not under the waiters' mutex, and a stale listener
  into whose backlog the next phase's peer connected.
\item \textbf{Multi-batch a2b.} The A2B mask bake derived the conv mask by casting transposed words to lanes,
  which holds only for 32-bit lanes; with 256-bit registers the a2b families classified 13--29 of 192
  images. The mask is now computed value by value with the A2B's own inverse packing, which leaves single-batch
  hashes unchanged, and the multi-batch a2b families classify 99--132 of 192 images, like the plain family.
\item \textbf{Multi-batch a2b with weights known in preprocessing.} The bake still excluded
  \flag{MODELWEIGHTS_KNOWN_DURING_PREPROCESSING} in multi-batch, so these builds ran the A2B without the bake, whose
  precomputed $[c]$ does not match the online mask: UC2 A2bits classified 2--4 of 32 CIFAR-10 images (the single-batch
  builds with the bake switched off likewise 0--2 of 10). P1's prescribed share $-r_1 = (m_1 \ll F) + \mathit{low}$ is
  now computed lane by lane (the same values with one word per register: single-batch hashes unchanged), the bake
  covers these builds, and they classify 20 of 32 (UC1 reshared 21 of 32).
\item \textbf{Threaded GEMM with \flag{A_KNOWN=0} or public weights.} The threaded GEMM had diverged from the serial one.
  With \flag{A_KNOWN=0} it sent the outputs without the conv triple's share and left the triple cursor behind, so
  every threaded build classified at chance (1 of 10). With public weights and \flag{TRUNC_DELAYED=0} it truncated the
  first layer with the regular truncation while the completion expected the data owner's exact one, and both parties
  waited forever in preprocessing. It now follows the serial path.
\item \textbf{BatchNorm triples with \flag{A_KNOWN=0}.} AB triples multiply the full masks, and the BN product's operand is
  $x - \mu$, whose mask includes the model owner's mask of $\mu$. The triple was set up over the masks of $x$, which left
  out $\lambda_s\lambda_\mu = s\mu$: every output was $s\cdot x + \beta$ (unit test failed, ResNet50 unfused 0 of 10).
  With \flag{A_KNOWN=1} only the data owner's mask share enters, and $\mu$ does not change it.
\item \textbf{Public weights with unfused BN and delayed truncation.} With public weights the first convolution's output is
  still the data owner's sharing (the other party's mask is zero). With \flag{TRUNC_DELAYED=1}, with or without
  \flag{BIT_INJECTION_TRUNC_SIM}, the BN truncated it with the SecureML local truncation, which wraps for positive values
  (0--1 of 10 correct). The owner now truncates it exactly and re-masks, in the same single message.
\item \textbf{Test harness.} The ReLU tests fed unbaked inputs to the reshare+sim skip (35\% wrong); the
  harness now declares its inputs unbaked.
\end{itemize}

After these fixes, all 60 threaded variants (30 single-batch, 30 multi-batch) reproduced their serial build's output hash on flare /
polynize (round 4), and repeated runs are bit-identical. The concurrent conv-triple evaluation keeps every single-batch hash
of round 4. The lane batching gives the multi-batch triples different random shares, so those hashes changed; its
correctness rests on the lane check and on the accuracy (\cref{sec:multi,fig:acc}). Hashes differ between the machine pairs, because the PRNG
draws 512-bit AES blocks with VAES-512 on Zen~4 and 256-bit blocks on Zen~3.

\section{Limitations and open problems}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Truncation.} ABY2 has no reduced-slack or exact truncation yet. It is not needed for accuracy with the
  AdamW model, but it would remove the truncation noise (\cref{sec:eval}).
\item \textbf{Weights known in preprocessing.} The multi-batch a2b bake still excludes
  \flag{MODELWEIGHTS_KNOWN_DURING_PREPROCESSING}, whose prescribed shares assume one word per register.
\item \textbf{Ciphertext occupancy.} At $7\times7$ feature maps, inputs are 5--19\% data and outputs
  6--24\%. Packing the sparse outputs (automorphisms, $N = 8192$ with a special prime) is implemented as the
  communication-optimized variant \flag{CHEETAH_CONV_REPACK} (round~4 in \cref{sec:triad}): 285 instead of 479\,MiB per HE
  product for 1.3--1.7\s more preprocessing, worth it below about 1--2\,Gbit/s. Packing the inputs remains open.
\item \textbf{A2B bake.} UC1 and UC2 still remask the 0.20 million ReLU inputs after the stem's pooling (1.5\,MiB in
  preprocessing). Committing the pooling's truncation mask, or a BatchNorm's output mask like a convolution's, would
  remove that; a BatchNorm bake works mechanically but changed the outputs of an unfused multi-batch check for reasons
  not yet understood, so it is off.
\item \textbf{OT extension.} On the CPU, ferret's LPN step and the AES key schedules of the correlation-robust hash
  are the largest costs of the Boolean preprocessing; a fixed-key hash is a security decision. On a GPU both run there
  (\cref{sec:gpu}); what remains on the CPU is the packs' setup (base OTs, IKNP, first extension), the latency-bound
  Boolean addition of the A2bits builds, and the few consumers that need the COT blocks themselves. The online phase
  measured 0.04--0.05\s higher with the GPU OT path in the UC1 builds on the shared workstation, for reasons not yet
  found; a two-machine GPU setup would settle it. In multi-batch the OT kernels of both parties saturate the one GPU.
\item \textbf{Multi-batch on one workstation.} With both parties on one machine, 62\,GB hold two GPU processes per party
  (16 ImageNet images) and one CPU process per party, so the 24-process configuration of the artifact cannot run there. The
  online activations scale linearly with the lanes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

The largest runtime gains came from doing less redundant work in the HE evaluator (packing, lazy
transforms, pipelining). The largest communication gains came from sending only information-bearing bits:
the exact wire format, full BatchNorm ciphertexts, and OTs whose message bits carry several products. In the online phase, latency rather than bandwidth
dominates, so the gains came from Nagle-free sockets, fewer rounds and parallelizing the per-round work at batch size
one. Seeded, bit-reproducible runs made every change measurable and exposed the bugs in \cref{sec:correct}.
Moving the A2B to preprocessing (\cref{sec:triad}) needs every ReLU input's mask fixed before the preprocessing pass;
most producers can commit their masks for free, and the care it takes is privacy bookkeeping: each committed mask may
mask one value only, and a mask narrowed for a truncation only where the other party never sees what it hides.
