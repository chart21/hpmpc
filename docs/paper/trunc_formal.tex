\subsection{TS1, TE1 and TE0 in ABY2: protocols and security}\label{sec:truncformal}

This subsection states the truncations of \cref{sec:triadts1,sec:triadte} as protocols in the style of Truncation
Untangled~\cite{tu}, proves the identities they compute and argues their security. All three run fused into a ReLU:
the input is a delayed convolution output $z$ at scale $2^{2F}$, the output $\mathrm{DReLU}(z)$ times the truncated
value, and neither sends a message beyond those of the ReLU's MSB adders and its bit injection.

\paragraph{Notation.} Values live in $\mathbb{Z}_{2^\ell}$ with $F$ fractional bits; $K = 2^{\ell-1-F}$. An ABY2 share
of $x$ is $\langle x \rangle = (m_x, [\lambda_x])$: the masked value $m_x$ is public to both parties, the mask
$\lambda_x = \lambda_x^0 + \lambda_x^1$ is additively shared (P$_i$ holds $\lambda_x^i$), and $x = m_x - \lambda_x$. For
the ReLU input we write $\nu = -\lambda_z = \nu_0 + \nu_1$ ($\nu_i = -\lambda_z^i$, the shares of the code's
\texttt{nu}), so $z = m + \nu$. A Boolean share is $\langle b \rangle^B = (m_b, [\lambda_b]^B)$ with
$b = m_b \oplus \lambda_b$; $[u]$ denotes additive shares of a bit $u$ in $\mathbb{Z}_{2^\ell}$, $[u]^B$ XOR shares,
$x[j]$ bit $j$ of $x$, $x \gg j$ the logical shift and $[\![P]\!] \in \{0, 1\}$ the indicator of $P$. The protocols call
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item $\mathcal{F}^{k}_{\mathrm{COT}}$: P0 inputs $x_0 \in \mathbb{Z}_{2^k}$, P1 a bit $x_1$; the outputs satisfy
  $c_0 + c_1 = x_0 x_1 \bmod 2^k$ (ferret's silent COT, \cref{sec:otformal}). $\mathrm{B2A}_k(u)$ turns $[u]^B$ into
  $[u] \bmod 2^{k+1}$ with one call: P$_i$ outputs $u_i - 2 c_i$, since $u_0 \oplus u_1 = u_0 + u_1 - 2 u_0 u_1$.
\item $\mathcal{F}^{k}_{\mathrm{MUX}}$: from $[b]^B$ and $[r]$, outputs $[b\,r] \bmod 2^k$ (two COTs, one per
  direction).
\item $\mathcal{F}^{B}_{\mathrm{ADD}}$ (the A2B bake's Boolean addition): P0 inputs $x_0$, P1 inputs $x_1$, both of
  $k$ bits; outputs XOR shares of every bit of $x_0 + x_1 \bmod 2^k$. Since bit $j$ of the sum is
  $x_0[j] \oplus x_1[j] \oplus \gamma_j$, each party's share of the carry $\gamma_j$ into bit $j$ is its share of the
  sum bit XOR its own input bit, without communication. The implementation is an RCA with one COT multiplication per
  AND gate.
\item $\Pi^{k}_{\mathrm{MSB}}(p, [x]^B)$: the a-known ROT-preprocessed MSB adder of width $k$ (RCA, PPA or four-way PPA,
  \cref{sec:otformal}) with a public summand $p$ and a Boolean-shared one; outputs
  $\langle \mathrm{MSB}(p + x \bmod 2^k) \rangle^B$. Its preprocessing (triples, tuples, output masks) does not depend on
  the inputs; online it opens the masked value of every gate output, each under a fresh mask.
\item $\Pi_{\mathrm{BI}}(\langle b \rangle^B, T)$: the bit injection of a bit into a value $T = M_T - \lambda_T$ given as
  a public part $M_T$ and $[\lambda_T]$, with $[\lambda_b]$ and $[\lambda_b \lambda_T]$ preprocessed. Locally
  $[b\,T] = m_b M_T - m_b [\lambda_T] + (2 m_b - 1)([\lambda_b \lambda_T] - M_T [\lambda_b])$ (from
  $b = m_b + (1 - 2 m_b)\lambda_b$); then each party adds its share of a fresh output mask and sends the sum, so both
  learn $m_{bT}$: one $\ell$-bit message per party, the ReLU's own message.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Correctness of $\Pi_{\mathrm{TS1}}$.} \textbf{Lemma 1.} If $y_1$, read as a signed $\ell'$-bit value, lies in
$[-2^{\ell'-2}, 2^{\ell'-2})$, then $T = \lfloor y_1 / 2^{t'} \rfloor$ for $t' = 0$ and
$T - \lfloor y_1 / 2^{t'} \rfloor \in \{0, 1\}$ for $t' > 0$; and $y_1 = \mathrm{round}(f z / 2^F) + 1 - E$ with
$E \in \{0, 1, 2\}$.

\emph{Proof.} The second claim: $f z = f m + f \nu_0 + f \nu_1 \bmod 2^\ell$, and shifting the three summands by $F$
separately loses the carries out of their low $F$ bits, $E \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ in all (the rounding constant moves
into $M_0$, the $+1$ centres the error). The first is TS1's identity~\cite[Fig.~9]{tu} in the ring $2^{\ell'}$ on
$x' = 2^{\ell'-2} - y_1 \in (0, 2^{\ell'-1}]$, whose masked value is $c = x' + \rho$ and whose mask $\rho$ has the
two shares $\rho_0, \rho_1$; it is the computation of Lemma~2 below with $\ell'$ for $\ell$, $t'$ for $F$ and offset
$2^{\ell'-2}$. Without $w_t$ the lift is exact for $t' = 0$ because $\lfloor y_1 / 2^0 \rfloor = y_1$ has no low bits
to carry out of. \hfill$\square$

A simulation of the formulas over $10^6$ random inputs ($\ell = 32$, $F = 5$, $f \in \{1, 4\}$; $f = 28$, $t' = 3$;
$\ell = 64$, $F = 12$) finds the lift exact (or $\{0, 1\}$) everywhere and $y - \mathrm{round}(f z / 2^{F+t'})$ in
$\{-1, 0, 1\}$; the unit test \texttt{RELU\_TS1} (4,096 inputs, all adders, 32 and 64 bits) agrees.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item $a = m \bmod 2^F$. In the same rounds:
  $\langle b \rangle^B = \neg\,\Pi^{\ell-F}_{\mathrm{MSB}}(m \gg F, [\nu[F..\ell{-}1]]^B)$, the DReLU of
  $A = (m \gg F) + (\nu \gg F) \bmod 2^{\ell-F}$ (the narrow adder on the top slices of the conversion), and
  $\langle c_t \rangle^B = (m_c, [\lambda_c]^B) = \Pi^{F+1}_{\mathrm{MSB}}(0 \| a, 0 \| [\nu[0..F{-}1]]^B)$, the carry into
  bit $F$ of $m + \nu$.
\item $c = -m + 2^{\ell-1}$, $s = 1 - 2 c[\ell-1]$, $c' = (c \gg F) \bmod 2^{\ell-1-F}$, $M = K - c' - K c[\ell-1]$;
  $[\![a \neq 0]\!] = (a + 2^F - 1) \gg F$; $s_c = 1 - 2 m_c$.
\item $T = M_T - \lambda_T$ with $M_T = M - [\![a \neq 0]\!] + m_c$,

  $[\lambda_b \lambda_T] = [\lambda_b l_a] + s K [\lambda_b r_{\mathrm{msb}}] - s_c [\lambda_b \lambda_c]$;

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Correctness of $\Pi_{\mathrm{TE1}}$ and $\Pi_{\mathrm{TE0}}$.} \textbf{Lemma 2.} For $z \in [0, 2^{\ell-1})$
and $\lambda_y = l_a + s K r_{\mathrm{msb}}$: $M - \lambda_y = \lfloor z / 2^F \rfloor + [\![a \neq 0]\!] - c_t$.
Hence $T = M_T - \lambda_T = \lfloor z / 2^F \rfloor$.

\emph{Proof.} Let $x' = 2^{\ell-1} - z \in (0, 2^{\ell-1}]$; then $c = -m + 2^{\ell-1} = x' + \nu \bmod 2^\ell$. Split
$c = 2^{\ell-1} c_h + c_l$ and $\nu = 2^{\ell-1} r_{\mathrm{msb}} + \nu_l$ with $c_l, \nu_l < 2^{\ell-1}$. As
$x' + \nu_l < 2^\ell$, its bit $\ell - 1$ is $c_h \oplus r_{\mathrm{msb}}$, so over the integers
$x' = c_l - \nu_l + 2^{\ell-1} (c_h + r_{\mathrm{msb}} - 2 c_h r_{\mathrm{msb}}) = c_l - \nu_l + 2^{\ell-1} c_h + 2^{\ell-1} s\,r_{\mathrm{msb}}$.
Dividing by $2^F$, $\lfloor x'/2^F \rfloor = \lfloor c_l/2^F \rfloor - \lfloor \nu_l/2^F \rfloor - \beta + K c_h + s K r_{\mathrm{msb}}$
with the borrow $\beta = [\![c \bmod 2^F < \nu \bmod 2^F]\!]$. The mask's low part is
$\nu_l = a_0 + a_1 - 2^{\ell-1} w$, so $\lfloor \nu_l / 2^F \rfloor = (a_0 \gg F) + (a_1 \gg F) + w_t - K w$. Substituting
$M = K - \lfloor c_l/2^F \rfloor - K c_h$ and $l_a$ gives $M - \lambda_y = K - \lfloor x'/2^F \rfloor - \beta$, and since
$\lfloor z / 2^F \rfloor = K - \lceil x'/2^F \rceil$,
$M - \lambda_y = \lfloor z / 2^F \rfloor + [\![x' \bmod 2^F \neq 0]\!] - \beta$. Finally let $\zeta = z \bmod 2^F$ and
$n = \nu \bmod 2^F$: $x' \bmod 2^F \neq 0$ iff $\zeta \neq 0$; $c \bmod 2^F = (n - \zeta) \bmod 2^F$, so
$\beta = [\![\zeta \neq 0 \wedge n \geq \zeta]\!]$; and $a = (\zeta - n) \bmod 2^F$ with $a + n = \zeta + 2^F c_t$. The
cases $\zeta = 0$, $n < \zeta$, $n = \zeta$ and $n > \zeta$ show that $[\![a \neq 0]\!] - c_t$ is 1 exactly when
$\zeta \neq 0$ and $n < \zeta$, and 0 otherwise, as is $[\![\zeta \neq 0]\!] - \beta$. With $c_t = m_c + s_c \lambda_c$, $M_T - \lambda_T = M - \lambda_y - [\![a \neq 0]\!] + c_t$.
\hfill$\square$

\textbf{Lemma 3.} With $b = \neg\,\mathrm{MSB}_{\ell-F}(A)$ as in $\Pi_{\mathrm{TE1}}$,
$b \cdot T = \mathrm{DReLU}(z) \lfloor z / 2^F \rfloor$ for $z \in [-2^{\ell-1} + 2^F, 2^{\ell-1})$.

\emph{Proof.} $m + \nu = z$ gives $A = \lfloor z / 2^F \rfloor - c_t \bmod 2^{\ell-F}$, and $K$ is half that ring. For
$z \geq 0$, $\lfloor z/2^F \rfloor \in [0, K)$, so $A \in [-1, K)$: $A = -1$ only if $\lfloor z/2^F \rfloor = 0$, where
$T = 0$ (Lemma~2) and the product is 0 for either bit; otherwise $b = 1$. For $z < 0$ in the range,
$\lfloor z/2^F \rfloor \in [-K + 1, -1]$, so $A \in [-K, -1]$ and $b = 0$, whatever $T$ is. In $\Pi_{\mathrm{TE0}}$,
$b = \mathrm{DReLU}(z)$ exactly. \hfill$\square$

Lemma~2 was also checked exhaustively for $\ell = 8$, $F \in \{2, 3\}$ (all $z \ge 0$ and all $\nu_0, \nu_1$) and by
simulation for $\ell \in \{16, 32, 64\}$; the unit tests \texttt{RELU\_TE1} / \texttt{RELU\_TE0} find every positive
input exact with all three adders at 32 and 64 bits.

\paragraph{Communication.} \Cref{tab:trunccost} counts what each scheme adds per ReLU value to the plain fused
ReLU (the MSB adder of the A2B and the bit injection's one $\ell$-bit message per party, the same in all of them).
Messages are those of the protocols above; an AND gate opens one bit per party, a COT of $k$ bits sends $k$ bits after
its random OT, a multiplexer of $k$ bits is two such COTs. TS\{L\} truncates the two parties' shares locally (SecureML), folded into
the multiplication's output exchange, and costs nothing in either phase; but it is probabilistic and needs slack:
for $|x| < 2^e$ the shares' truncation fails with probability up to $2^{e+1-\ell}$, an error of about $2^{\ell-F}$. TS1 removes the wraps for 22 bits of COT material per value
and nothing online. TE1 makes the result exact for about 5 times that in preprocessing (the full-width Boolean
addition, three COTs of $\ell - 1$ bits) and the low adder's 4 messages online, which run inside the DReLU adder's
rounds; TE0 additionally pays the cut. Measured on ImageNet, $8.8$ million values take the TE schemes' online
messages: 4 per value, 8.4\,MiB, as \cref{tab:trunc} shows.

\paragraph{Security.} \textbf{Theorem 2.} $\Pi_{\mathrm{TS1}}$, $\Pi_{\mathrm{TE1}}$ and $\Pi_{\mathrm{TE0}}$ securely
compute their outputs against a semi-honest adversary corrupting one party, in the hybrid model with
$\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{COT}}$, $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{MUX}}$, $\mathcal{F}^{B}_{\mathrm{ADD}}$ and the preprocessing of
$\Pi_{\mathrm{MSB}}$.

\emph{Proof sketch.} \emph{Offline.} Every value a party receives is an output of an ideal functionality, which
hands it a share that is uniform on its own (the other party's share completes it); the local steps (shifts,
$l_a$, the carries' shares) are functions of its view. The simulator draws these shares uniformly. \emph{Online.}
The messages are the opened masked values of the gates of the MSB adders (one adder in $\Pi_{\mathrm{TS1}}$, two in
$\Pi_{\mathrm{TE1}}$ and $\Pi_{\mathrm{TE0}}$) and the bit injection's $m_{bT}$. Each is the sum of a value and a fresh
mask of which the corrupted party holds one share and the other share is uniform and used nowhere else: the gates'
output masks are drawn independently in preprocessing, $\lambda_c$ and $\lambda_b$ are independent of each other and
of $\nu$ (the cross terms of $d$ go through $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{COT}}$), and the bit injection's output mask is fresh.
So every opened value is uniform given the rest of the view, and the simulator sends uniform values (for $m_{bT}$, the
masked output it derives from the corrupted party's output share and mask share). The public quantities $M_0$, $c$,
$s$, $M$, $a$, $[\![a \neq 0]\!]$ and $m_c$ are functions of opened values. The bake's committed masks are as in the
rest of the A2bits protocol (one value per slot, \cref{sec:triad}). \hfill$\square$
