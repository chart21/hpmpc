\section{The all\_opt configurations on ImageNet}\label{sec:triad}

The artifact's single-batch 2PC configurations (\texttt{measurements/configs/artifacts/triad/2pc/single\_batch}) combine
the online optimizations of the protocol: six configurations, A2bits and reshared, each in the three use cases of
\cref{sec:setting}: UC1, weights known to none (\flag{A_KNOWN=0}); UC2, weights known to the model owner
(\flag{A_KNOWN=1}, here also known in preprocessing, \flag{MODELWEIGHTS_KNOWN_DURING_PREPROCESSING=1}); and UC3,
weights known to all (public weights). Each runs ResNet50 on one ImageNet image with the three adders
(\flag{FUNCTION_IDENTIFIER=87,187,287}) and with \flag{COMPRESS=0,1}: 36 builds. As in the configurations, the model and
the image are dummies (\flag{MODELOWNER=-1}, \flag{DATAOWNER=-1}); the runtime does not depend on their values.

\paragraph{Builds.} \emph{As given}: the configuration unchanged (hpmpc \texttt{161cf1d}); its conv triples use
CHEETAH's \texttt{HomConv2DSS}. \emph{Optimized}: the configuration plus every optimization of this report
(hpmpc \texttt{1421df0}, ConvTriple \texttt{5b568e9}): \flag{CHEETAH_CONV_PACKED=1} with the pipeline, \flag{CUT_FRACTIONAL_BITS_OPT=1},
\flag{ADDITIONAL_RELU_THREADS=24} (equal to the configuration's \flag{ADDITIONAL_GEMM_THREADS}, as the two share one
pool), 100,000-word buffers, and \flag{A2B_CONV_BAKE=1} for the A2bits configurations; everything
else of \cref{sec:he,sec:ot,sec:online} is on by default. \flag{RNG_AHEAD=1}, part of the optimized set until this
last round, is left out: at the final code it saves the Zen~4 hosts about 0.04\s of online time (median over the 36
builds) but costs the Zen~3 hosts up to 0.17\s (UC3, \flag{COMPRESS=1}: 0.51 against 0.36\s); a profile puts that in
the online draws, most likely waiting for the ring's producer thread. Each
build runs three times per machine pair (median): the as-given builds alternating with the final code, the optimized
builds in a separate series.

\paragraph{Correctness.} The same flags on CIFAR-10 with the AdamW model and real data (P0 / P1 as owners, 10
images) classify 4--7 images correctly for every configuration with \flag{COMPRESS=0} (plaintext: 6), including all
UC2 builds, and every output hash is unchanged by the changes below. \flag{COMPRESS=1}
evaluates the ReLU on bits 12--19 only (\flag{REDUCED_BITLENGTH_m=12}, \flag{k=20}). With \flag{FRACTIONAL=5} the
carry dropped from the 12 low bits flips the sign of activations below 128.0, and even the plain RCA build without any of
our changes classifies 0 of 10. It is a runtime setting only, and we keep it running rather than accurate. Its A2bits
builds aborted with the element-parallel levels: the A2B bake committed conv masks for 8 of the 32 slices, so most
outputs drew fresh masks. The bake now requires a full-width ReLU, and \flag{COMPRESS=1} runs the A2B unbaked.

The A2bits configurations \emph{as given} are not correct. They set \flag{A2B_ONLINE_OPT=1}, which precomputes the
A2B's secret operand $[c] = \mathrm{bool}(-\lambda)$ by a Boolean addition in preprocessing, but not the bake
(\flag{A2B_CONV_BAKE}), and without it the preprocessing pass generates the MSB adder's Beaver triples for the
Boolean addition's input while the online phase uses its output $[c]$. On CIFAR-10 with the AdamW model, the as-given
A2bits flags classify 0--2 of 10 images in all three use cases and every adder, as do the optimized flags without
the bake (same output hashes as as-given in UC3); with the bake they classify 4--7. The reshared configurations do not
use \flag{A2B_ONLINE_OPT} and classify 4--7 of 10 as given.

\paragraph{Why the bake remasks ReLU inputs.} A ReLU on $m = v + \lambda$ runs its MSB adder on $s_1 = \mathrm{bool}(m)$,
public, and $s_2 = \mathrm{bool}(-\lambda)$, secret, since $\mathrm{bool}(m) \boxplus \mathrm{bool}(-\lambda) =
\mathrm{bool}(v)$. \flag{A2B_ONLINE_OPT} computes $[c] = \mathrm{bool}(-\lambda)$ in preprocessing, from the parties'
$\mathrm{bool}(-\lambda_0)$ and $\mathrm{bool}(-\lambda_1)$ by a Boolean addition (31 levels of AND gates). Two facts fix
\emph{when} this can happen. The adder's Beaver triples are defined by the masks of its input wires, $[c]$ among them,
and are requested while the preprocessing pass walks the circuit. And that pass is a single non-interactive sweep that
fixes masks, sends one-way messages and records triple requests; the triples are generated in bulk afterwards. So $[c]$
cannot be computed inside the pass, which would need 31 interactive rounds mid-sweep. It cannot be computed after the
pass either, which is the as-given configuration: its triples then belong to a different mask. It must exist
\emph{before} the pass, and with it every ReLU input's mask $\lambda$.

The bake therefore commits the masks first. Each party $i$ draws a random $\ell_i$ per ReLU input, the Boolean addition
runs on $\mathrm{bool}(-\ell_i)$ before the pass, and the pass then has to deliver every ReLU input under the mask
$\ell = \ell_0 + \ell_1$. Whether that is free depends on the layer that produces the input. A convolution or FC layer
with secret weights (UC1, UC2) ends with a message that re-masks its output under a mask each party draws: the party
draws $\ell_i$ instead (with weights known in preprocessing, P1's triple share is chosen to produce it). Every other
producer fixes $\lambda$ without such a choice: a convolution with public weights is local, so its output mask is
$W \ast \lambda_{\mathrm{in}}$, a linear function of the previous bit injections' masks; a residual addition gives
$\lambda_a + \lambda_b$; pooling is linear too, and the BatchNorm layers that follow them do not commit their output
masks in this implementation. Such an input is moved onto the committed mask:
party $i$ sends $\delta_i = \ell_i - \lambda_i$ in preprocessing, and online both parties add $\delta_0 + \delta_1$ to
$m$, which gives $v + \ell$. Since $\ell_i$ is uniform and known only to party $i$, $\delta_i$ is a one-time pad of
$\lambda_i$ and reveals nothing. The price is one ring element per value and party, in preprocessing only.

In the CHEETAH ResNet50 layout, 7.30 of the 9.01 million ReLU inputs come straight from a convolution (with BatchNorm
fused into it); only the ReLUs after the stem's pooling and after the four residual additions (1.71 million) need the
remask in UC1 and UC2, 13\,MiB. In UC3 every convolution is local, so all 9.01 million do: $9.01\cdot 10^6 \cdot 2
\cdot 4$ bytes $= 69$\,MiB, exactly the difference in \cref{fig:triadcomm}. Avoiding it would take the actual masks
before the Boolean addition: a second, mask-only pass over the network ahead of it. We estimate that pass at about the
single-threaded time of the preprocessing pass (0.3--0.5\s), to save 69\,MiB, 22\,ms at 25\,Gbit/s. Alternatively the
previous layer's bit-injection masks could be chosen so that $W \ast \lambda_{\mathrm{in}} = \ell$, but that is a linear
system over $\mathbb{Z}_{2^{32}}$ without a solution in general.

\paragraph{Where the time goes.} Preprocessing is bound by OT generation: in a CPU profile over all threads, ferret's LPN
step takes 31--44\%, the MITCCRH key schedules 8--11\% and MPCOT 10--13\%. Per A2bits RCA inference the OT packs'
setup costs 0.9\s, the bake's Boolean addition 1.5\s (31 carry levels over 9.0 million values; one level pays a ferret
extension of all 32 packs), the conv triples 0.7\s and the network's preprocessing pass 0.5\s. PPA4 adds 3.1\s of Beaver
tuples. After the changes below, 84\% of the online CPU samples are idle workers: RCA's 1,714 rounds at about 0.3\ms each
are close to its floor, and disabling the NIC's interrupt coalescing, which cut the ping from 0.25 to 0.08\ms, shortened
the RCA online phase by only 7\%.

\paragraph{Changes.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Worker wake-ups.} Between two circuit levels a worker spun about 100\,\textmu s and then slept on a
  futex, shorter than a round trip, so every level woke 24 threads. Workers now wait about 20\ms, on the counter's own
  cache line: with the counter next to the completion count, every completion invalidated the line for all spinning
  workers, which slowed the Zen~3 hosts' UC3 builds by up to 35\%. On AMD CPUs the wait uses
  MONITORX/MWAITX, which parks the thread on the cache line without taking issue slots from its SMT sibling.
\item \textbf{Vectorized GEMM accumulation} (\flag{A_KNOWN=1}). The online GEMM called a share-level kernel per
  multiply-accumulate on $\{m, \lambda\}$ structs, about 0.2 MAC per cycle. The kernel of each variant is a sum of plain
  ring products, so it now runs as $C = XY^\top$ on \texttt{uint32} matrices with a register-blocked $4\times 32$
  kernel (AVX-512 or AVX2), with the weight operand cached across a layer's calls. The results are bit-identical; with
  weights known in preprocessing, P1's accumulation, which its mask/send never reads, is skipped. The conv layers'
  online time falls by a quarter on Zen~3; on Zen~4 the accumulation was not on the critical path.
\item \textbf{The A2B bake} is restricted to full-width ReLUs (above).
\item \textbf{Conv triples alongside the preprocessing pass} (\flag{CHEETAH_CONV_ASYNC}). The conv triples used to
  start after the pass, whose single thread fixes their input masks. Now the batched HE pipeline starts on its own thread
  together with the pass and takes each layer as soon as the pass has recorded its masks; the rest of the preprocessing
  waits for it before the next generator uses the channels. Only the layers the pass reaches last leave a tail.
\item \textbf{Parallel preprocessing levels} (\flag{STREAM_PARALLEL_PRE}). The A2B, adder and bit-injection levels of
  the pass run on the worker pool like online. The pass's append-only streams (triple types and triple inputs, the
  pre-send buffer, stored outputs) get per-worker cursors, reads use the online cursors, and every worker's use of each
  stream is checked against the first element's. The GEMM levels stay serial. Both changes leave all 18 CIFAR-10 output
  hashes and two multi-batch hashes unchanged.
\end{itemize}
More HE/OT threads help the OT-heavy builds: \flag{CHEETAH_THREADS=64} instead of the configurations' 32 (64 OT packs)
shortens preprocessing by 8\% (median) on Zen~4 and 4\% on Zen~3, by up to 20\% for PPA4, but lengthens it by up to 30\%
for the UC3 RCA builds, where setting up 64 packs costs more than it saves. Two ferret threads per pack,
busy-polling the receive path and 31 instead of 24 workers did not help.

\paragraph{Results.} \Cref{fig:triad} shows all 36 builds; \cref{tab:triad} summarizes them. At the final code every
build is faster than as given, in both phases and on both pairs. In UC1 and UC2 the packed, pipelined and concurrent
conv triples running alongside the parallel preprocessing pass, the exact wire format, the shared-OT tuples and
\flag{TCP_NODELAY} cut preprocessing from 11.7--17.3\s to 1.9--6.2\s on the Zen~4 pair ($2.6$--$6.7\times$) and from
15.1--21.9\s to 2.7--7.8\s on the Zen~3 pair ($2.8$--$6.0\times$). The online phase falls from 0.77--1.55\s to
0.39--0.70\s ($1.8$--$2.8\times$; Zen~3: 0.72--1.73 to 0.27--0.70\s, $2.0$--$3.8\times$). In UC2, with the weights
known in preprocessing, the online phase takes 0.39--0.62\s on Zen~4 and 0.27--0.54\s on Zen~3.

UC3 needs no linear-layer triples, so its preprocessing is OT-bound and changes least: $1.0$--$1.2\times$ faster on
both pairs, with the online phase $1.4$--$1.9\times$ faster ($1.4$--$2.1\times$ on Zen~3). Before the parallel
preprocessing levels, the Zen~3 hosts' A2bits builds with \flag{COMPRESS=1} preprocessed 0.11--0.14\s \emph{slower} than
as given (five interleaved runs each). A one-flag-off ablation placed all of it in \flag{ADDITIONAL_RELU_THREADS}, whose
per-call stream-cursor checks slowed P0's last preprocessing step from 0.32--0.34 to 0.44--0.49\s. Unchecked versions
for the preprocessing share removed that but slowed the \flag{COMPRESS=0} builds by as much, a code-layout effect we
did not keep. The parallel preprocessing levels now more than make up for it. As-given A2bits also skips the 69\,MiB
rebase of the A2B bake and is not correct (Correctness).

\paragraph{Communication.} \Cref{fig:triadcomm} shows the traffic of the same builds as P0 counts it; P1's counters
mirror P0's, and the reruns and the Zen~3 pair send the same bytes. In UC1 and UC2 the optimized builds send
$1.35$--$1.86\times$ less in preprocessing with \flag{COMPRESS=0} (1,323--3,225 to 790--2,150\,MiB) and
$1.61$--$1.92\times$ less with \flag{COMPRESS=1} (1,219--2,353 to 696--1,308\,MiB). The conv triples account for
nearly all of it: in UC2 they fall from about 1,000 to 479\,MiB (\cref{tab:heformal}), in UC1, whose AB triples need
both cross products, from 2,003 to 958\,MiB. With the weights known in preprocessing, both UC2 builds also send P1's
share corrections, 4 bytes per conv output (42\,MiB). The OT-based tuples are unchanged, except that with
\flag{CUT_FRACTIONAL_BITS_OPT} PPA4 needs fewer multi-input AND tuples: 12\,MiB less for reshared and 74\,MiB less
for A2bits, where the bake lets the cut skip the $b$-product gates. The network's preprocessing pass changes by
$-67$ to $+8$\,MiB in UC1 and UC2. UC3 needs no linear-layer triples, and its traffic is unchanged except in the
A2bits builds with \flag{COMPRESS=0}, where the A2B bake adds 69\,MiB to the preprocessing pass. There, no ReLU input
comes straight from a masked conv layer, so the bake rebases every one of the 9.0 million inputs onto its committed
mask, one 32-bit word per value and party. The as-given builds skip that rebase, which is why they send less, and
compute wrong signs (see Correctness). Online, \flag{CUT_FRACTIONAL_BITS_OPT} removes 4--8\% of the traffic of the
UC1 and UC2 builds with \flag{COMPRESS=0} (A2bits PPA4: none). The cut needs \flag{TRUNC_DELAYED=0}, so it does not
apply to UC3; with \flag{COMPRESS=1} the online traffic does not change.

\paragraph{What is left.} \Cref{tab:triadphases} splits the time of six builds at the final code, and CPU profiles add
where the threads spend it. Before the last two changes the preprocessing pass took 0.43--0.86\s over the 18 builds
and the conv triples (0.71--0.84\s) followed it; now the pass takes 0.25--0.64\s and the conv triples run alongside.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{OT generation} remains the largest cost. Every build pays 0.82--0.93\s to set up the 32 ferret packs;
  the tuples after that take 0.24--0.37\s for RCA but 1.5--3.5\s for PPA4, whose Beaver 3- and 4-tuples need up to six
  more extension rounds of all 32 packs. Ferret's LPN step takes 33--41\% of all CPU samples, the AES key schedules of the
  correlation-robust hash 8--11\%. The last extension round is only partly used, but that wastes at most one round
  (about 0.3\s for PPA4). A fixed-key hash would remove most of the key schedules, but that is a security decision
  (\cref{sec:limits}).
\item \textbf{The bake's Boolean addition} costs the A2bits builds 1.5\s: 31 dependent COT-multiplication rounds of
  15--22\ms plus an extension round. Preparing its inputs costs another 0.25\s of serial time (column other). A
  log-depth adder would need 5--6 rounds but more AND gates, and so more OTs.
\item \textbf{The conv triples' tail.} In UC1 and UC2 the HE still runs 0.28--0.60\s past the pass, and 1.0--1.1\s
  for reshared PPA4, where it proceeds at about half its serial speed next to the pass: the pass's worker levels and the
  HE's 32 threads share the 32 cores. The layers the pass reaches last can only start at its end; committing their
  input masks early, as the bake does for the conv outputs, would let all layers start at once.
\item \textbf{Serial work} in the pass: the GEMM levels (the weights-known-in-preprocessing path pushes into vectors
  there) and the bake's input preparation still run on one thread.
\item \textbf{Online}, RCA's 1,714--2,012 rounds cost 0.14--0.18\s of waiting. Of the rest, the main thread spends
  0.11\s handing levels to the pool, 0.05\s transposing bits for the A2B serially, and 0.06\s in layer glue (copies,
  residual additions, freeing buffers).
\item \textbf{Communication:} output repacking could halve the conv-triple traffic (\cref{sec:heformal}), at a CPU cost
  that only pays off on slower links.
\end{enumerate}
