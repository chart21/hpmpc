1. WHAT WORKS (verified in this session, clean rebuilds for every cell)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. The arithmetic-to-boolean conversion bake
   (A2B_ONLINE_OPT=1 + A_KNOWN_TO_EVALUATORS_OPT=1 + A2B_CONV_BAKE=1,
    which implies RESHARE_OPT=0 and RESHARE_OPT_SIM=0)

   The bake commits every convolution / fully-connected output mask before the
   preprocessing and online passes, runs the boolean addition that produces the
   precomputed boolean share of the negated mask EARLY (at the same generation
   stage as the multiplication triples), and feeds the committed values
   identically into both passes. This makes the online-optimized
   arithmetic-to-boolean conversion correct, which it previously was not.

   Verified support matrix (unit tests / LeNet accuracy):

   | Weight setting                                | TRUNC_DELAYED=0 | TRUNC_DELAYED=1 |
   |-----------------------------------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
   | MODELWEIGHTS_KNOWN_DURING_PREPROCESSING=1     | 8 of 8 / 90%    | 6 of 8 / 90%    |
   | MODELWEIGHTS_KNOWN_DURING_PREPROCESSING=0     | 8 of 8 / 90%    | 6 of 8 / 90%    |
   | A_KNOWN=0                                     | 8 of 8 / 90%    | 6 of 8 / 90%    |

   90 percent equals the plain (no optimization) baseline accuracy in every
   cell. The reason for "6 of 8" is explained in section 3 below; it is a
   property of the delayed-truncation configuration, not a defect.

   CUT_FRACTIONAL_BITS_OPT=1 now also works together with the bake (section 4).

B. The reshare-simulation family
   (RESHARE_OPT=1 + RESHARE_OPT_SIM=1, together with CUT_FRACTIONAL_BITS_OPT=1)

   | Setting                                       | Unit tests | LeNet |
   |-----------------------------------------------|------------|-------|
   | MODELWEIGHTS_KNOWN_DURING_PREPROCESSING=0     | 8 of 8     | 90%   |
   | MODELWEIGHTS_KNOWN_DURING_PREPROCESSING=1     | 8 of 8     | 90%   |
   | PUBLIC_WEIGHTS=1 with TRUNC_DELAYED=1         | 6 of 8     | 90%   |

   Note that under PUBLIC_WEIGHTS=1 the configuration automatically falls back
   to RESHARE_OPT_SIM=0 (documented fallback in config.h); the two unit-test
   failures in that row are again the delayed-truncation artifacts of section 3.

C. A_KNOWN=0 baseline (fixed in this session; previously 0 of 8 and 10 percent)
   Three independent pre-existing bugs were found and fixed:
   - The tiled convolution send retrieves its multiplication-triple output
     INDEXED (reads at cursor position plus index without advancing the
     cursor), so a cursor advance by the layer size must follow; that advance
     was wrongly restricted to A_KNOWN=1. Under A_KNOWN=0 the cursor stayed at
     the convolution block and every later retrieval read shifted values,
     which made every test after the convolution test fail with garbage.
   - The network's first layer consumes the raw data-owner input, whose public
     value part is zero under SHARE_PREP=1. That makes the truncation share
     pair equal to the bare layer-triple output shares, whose integer sum
     systematically wraps for negative outputs: every negative output came out
     too large by exactly two to the power (BITLENGTH minus FRACTIONAL). The
     first layer's input is now re-randomized in place before the layer runs.
   - The batch-normalization dot product always used the accumulation variant
     for the "mask of the first factor is known" triple flavor, even under
     A_KNOWN=0 where the triples are of the symmetric flavor. It now
     dispatches on A_KNOWN exactly like the general matrix multiplication.

D. MaxPool correctness under BOTH optimization families (fixed this session)
   The MaxPool unit test previously used the global comparison tolerance of
   0.8 while all candidate values lie within 0.8 of every window maximum, so
   even a wrong candidate pick could not fail the test - the test was
   meaningless. It now compares against the fixed-point-quantized expected
   value with a tolerance of 0.01. The honest test exposed that maximum
   pooling comparisons were silently broken under BOTH baking schemes
   (consistent but wrong sign bits lead to wrong candidate picks). Two fixes:
   - The comparison differences are re-masked through the baked path before
     the sign computation, so the committed material matches the values.
   - Under the reshare simulation the comparison adders now run with the
     fractional-bits cut active (as the rectified-linear-unit already did),
     because the reshare bake's stride and slot mapping are compile-time
     cut-aware; an uncut adder consumed thirty-one instead of twenty-six
     random multiplications per adder and misaligned every group.

2. WHAT IS BROKEN OR NOT SUPPORTED (known, documented)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- PUBLIC_WEIGHTS=1 together with the arithmetic-to-boolean bake: not
  supported. Under public weights even the test re-masking helper takes a
  different code path, so no mask is committed at all; unit tests drop to
  3 of 8 and LeNet to 0 percent. Public weights currently require the
  reshare-simulation family (row B above), which handles them via its
  documented fallback. Supporting the bake here would require solving the
  "choose the party-one mask through the public weight matrix" problem
  (same blocker class as the deferred PUBLIC_WEIGHTS prescription task).

- COMPUTE_ARGMAX=1 (the final argument-maximum computed inside the secure
  computation instead of on revealed outputs): has the same
  unbaked-comparison structure that MaxPool had, and would need the same
  re-mask-plus-cut treatment. All current tests reveal the logits and take
  the argument maximum in the clear, so this is not exercised.

- The plain boolean AND test in the basic-primitives suite
  (FUNCTION_IDENTIFIER 54) fails under EVERY configuration, including the
  plain baseline with A_KNOWN=1. This is a pre-existing break of the plain
  boolean multiplication-triple path, is independent of every optimization
  discussed here, and is not exercised by the convolution/pooling suite or
  by LeNet (whose boolean gates all go through the adder and correlated-
  oblivious-transfer paths). Left open.

- The exact-truncation work for A_KNOWN=0 (pre-truncated dealer triples)
  lives on a different branch. On this branch A_KNOWN=0 relies on the
  first-layer re-randomization workaround described in section 1.C, which is
  correct but costs one extra multiplication triple and one communication
  round for the first layer's inputs.

- The parallel-prefix-adder families under the bake accept the cut flag but
  only the ripple-carry adder actually skips gates (section 4).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHY SOME UNIT-TEST RUNS REPORT "PASSED 6 OUT OF 8"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every "6 of 8" in the tables above comes from configurations with
TRUNC_DELAYED=1, and the two failing tests are always the standalone
Convolution test and the standalone BatchNorm test. This is EXPECTED
behavior of that configuration, not a correctness bug. Detailed reasoning:

Fixed-point arithmetic represents a real number x as the integer
x times two to the power FRACTIONAL (here FRACTIONAL = 5, so a scale factor
of thirty-two). When two fixed-point numbers are multiplied, the product
carries twice the scale factor (two to the power ten instead of two to the
power five), so every multiplication must be followed by a truncation
(division by two to the power FRACTIONAL) to return to the normal scale.

TRUNC_DELAYED=1 is an optimization that intentionally POSTPONES this
truncation: the convolution or fully-connected layer leaves its outputs at
the doubled scale, and the truncation is folded into the next
rectified-linear-unit, where it can be combined with the sign-bit
computation and the bit injection at no extra cost. In a real network every
convolution is followed by such an activation, so the postponed truncation
is always caught up before anyone looks at the values.

The standalone Convolution test and the standalone BatchNorm test, however,
reveal the layer output DIRECTLY, with no activation following. Under
TRUNC_DELAYED=1 the revealed values are therefore still at the doubled
scale: the test prints values that are exactly thirty-two times larger than
the expected values (for example, expected 0.1625, got 5.5). The comparison
fails, and the suite reports "Passed 6 out of 8".

That this is an artifact and not a bug is confirmed by three observations:
first, the same two tests pass in the same configuration with
TRUNC_DELAYED=0, where the layer truncates immediately (8 of 8); second,
every test that contains an activation after the layer (fully-connected
followed by rectifier, convolution followed by rectifier, rectifier with
average pooling) passes under TRUNC_DELAYED=1, because the delayed
truncation is correctly caught up inside the activation; third, the
unmodified no-optimization baseline shows exactly the same 6 of 8 pattern
under TRUNC_DELAYED=1, and LeNet - whose layers are all
activation-terminated - reaches the full 90 percent accuracy in these very
configurations.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. CUT_FRACTIONAL_BITS_OPT together with the arithmetic-to-boolean bake
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Background: under TRUNC_DELAYED=0 the inputs of a rectified-linear-unit have
already been truncated, so their absolute value is below two to the power
(BITLENGTH minus FRACTIONAL minus one). Consequently the top FRACTIONAL bit
positions of the value are pure sign extension, and the sign can be read at
the boundary bit position instead of the topmost bit position. The
fractional-bits cut exploits this by stopping the adder early and skipping
the gates for the redundant top slices.

Previously the cut's eligibility condition required RESHARE_OPT=1, so under
the bake the flag was silently inert. The eligibility now has a second leg
for A2B_ONLINE_OPT=1 + A_KNOWN_TO_EVALUATORS_OPT=1, and:

- The ripple-carry adder family for the bake (the "a known to evaluators"
  circuit) received the full cut: the carry chain stops at the boundary
  slice, the most significant bit is read there, the beaver-triple retrieval
  matches the reduced gate count, and FRACTIONAL fewer communication rounds
  and triples are spent per adder. Verified: unit tests 8 of 8 and LeNet at
  90 percent with the cut active.
- The parallel-prefix adder and the four-way parallel-prefix adder for the
  bake are CUT-COMPATIBLE but not yet cut-optimized: they ignore the runtime
  cut flag and compute the full circuit, which remains correct because under
  the bake no external stream count depends on adder-internal gates (the
  precomputed boolean share, the public summand, and the early boolean
  addition all stay at full width). Porting the gate-skipping identity
  substitution into these two generated circuit families (the same work that
  was done for the reshare family earlier) is left as follow-up; it is an
  efficiency improvement only. Verified cut-compatible: unit tests 8 of 8
  and LeNet at 90 percent with the parallel-prefix adder and the cut flag
  set.
- The reshare-family leg of the eligibility condition is unchanged;
  its configurations still pass 8 of 8 after the change.

      re-masking treatment (re-randomize the rectifier input through the baked
      path when it is not a direct convolution output) is the fix, left as
      follow-up. LeNet-style networks (pure convolution / fully-connected
      chains into rectifiers) remain fully supported by both families.

7. WHY FUSED CONVOLUTION-BATCHNORM NEEDS HIGHER BITLENGTH AND PRECISION

   Fusing folds the batch-normalization scale gamma divided by the square root
   of (variance plus epsilon) INTO the convolution weights:
   fused weight = weight times scale. Measured over all 23.45 million
   convolution weights and 26,560 batch-normalization channels of the ResNet50
   model:

   - The scale is below one for 99.3 percent of channels; median 0.033;
     90 percent of channels are below 0.25.
   - YES, fusion makes the weights MUCH smaller: the median absolute weight
     drops from 0.0756 (raw) to 0.0016 (fused) - a factor of about
     FORTY-EIGHT.
   - Consequence for fixed-point representation (weights quantizing to zero):
       FRACTIONAL= 5: raw 30.7%  -> fused 89.3%
       FRACTIONAL= 8: raw  4.0%  -> fused 54.2%
       FRACTIONAL=10: raw  1.0%  -> fused 28.7%
       FRACTIONAL=12: raw  0.3%  -> fused 11.0%
     So the fused network needs roughly ten to fourteen fractional bits before
     the weights survive quantization at all.
   - At the same time each multiplication carries TWICE FRACTIONAL fractional
     bits before truncation, so at thirty-two bits the integer headroom
     vanishes as FRACTIONAL grows: empirically accuracy already collapses at
     FRACTIONAL=10 (overflow and truncation-wrap), BEFORE the fused weights
     become representable. The two requirements cannot both be met in a
     thirty-two-bit word: fused batch normalization therefore needs
     BITLENGTH=64 (which the two-party adder circuits do not provide yet -
     there are no sixty-four-bit specializations of the sign-bit adders).
   - The unfused path splits the problem: the raw weights survive five
     fractional bits, and the tiny scale is applied as a separate per-channel
     multiplier to LARGE (unnormalized) inputs, so the product is well above
     the quantization step. Its price is integer range - batch-normalization
     running means reach 218, and the unnormalized activations consume the
     headroom that higher FRACTIONAL would need, which is why the unfused
     accuracy drops again at FRACTIONAL=8. At thirty-two bits the working
     combination is: UNFUSED batch normalization with FRACTIONAL=5.
